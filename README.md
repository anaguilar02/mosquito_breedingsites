# mosquito_breedingsites
## Abstract
Mosquito-borne diseases are an increasing public health concern, and urban drainage infrastructure such as storm drains and catch basins can provide breeding habitat for Aedes vectors, including Aedes albopictus. Detecting these sites over large areas is costly with traditional surveillance alone, and citizen science offers a complementary, lower-cost source of geolocated observations. Mosquito Alert is a citizen science platform, active since 2014, through which participants report adult mosquitoes, bites, and potential breeding sites. In this work, we present an exploratory visual analysis of storm drain breeding sites reported through the platform. We used the two storm drain categories available in the application (storm drains with water and dry storm drains), covering the period from the start of the project in 2014 to August 2026, and obtained the records from the project’s public map. We counted the reported sites by country and geographic coordinate, examined how often stagnant water is reported, compared the presence of stagnant water with the presence of larvae, and mapped the records globally, with particular attention to areas with the highest number of reports. We also developed an interactive map to help inspect regions where many records overlap. Our aim is not to model mosquito populations but to describe the structure of the data and highlight patterns worth further investigation. Because citizen science reporting effort varies across space and socioeconomic groups, we discuss how spatial sampling bias and other limitations of volunteer-based data affect the interpretation of these patterns.

Keywords: citizen science, Mosquito Alert, storm drains, breeding sites, Aedes albopictus, exploratory data analysis, data visualization


## What can you find here?
breedingsites_map.html contains the interactive map to visualize the breeding sites identified by Mosquito Alert. It also contains a second map showing the correlation between stagnant water and larvae.

Data was taken from Mosquito Alert public map (https://map.mosquitoalert.com) and is found under the name "breeding_sites.csv".
